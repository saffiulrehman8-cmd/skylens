Global
Circulation

Jet streams, trade winds, and the Coriolis effect explained.

Earth's atmosphere organizes into six large circulation cells driven by differential solar heating. Hadley Cells dominate the tropics: warm equatorial air rises, flows poleward, descends at the subtropics creating deserts, and returns as steady Trade Winds.

Ferrel Cells (30°–60°) produce the dominant Westerlies — the winds steering most mid-latitude weather. The Coriolis effect deflects all moving air rightward in the Northern Hemisphere. The polar jet stream meanders at 30,000ft and 320 km/h, guiding entire weather systems across continents.

💨 Jet Streams: Commercial aircraft exploit jet streams to save 2+ hours and significant fuel on transoceanic eastbound flights.
🌀 Coriolis Myth: The Coriolis effect is far too weak to decide which way your bathtub drains — it only meaningfully shapes large-scale systems like cyclones and jet streams, not sink-sized ones.
