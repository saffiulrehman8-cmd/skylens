Hail &
Precipitation Types

How raindrops freeze into hailstones, and what separates sleet from freezing rain.

Related Topics

Severe Storms
Tornadoes
Global Circulation

Hail forms inside strong thunderstorm updrafts, where a small ice pellet is carried repeatedly through freezing upper layers of the storm. Each pass adds another thin layer of ice, similar to an onion — the more cycles, the larger the stone grows, until the updraft can no longer support its weight and it falls to the ground.

Not all frozen precipitation is hail. Sleet forms when snow partially melts, then refreezes into small ice pellets before reaching the ground. Freezing rain stays entirely liquid through the air and only freezes on contact with a surface below 0°C — the cause of the most dangerous ice storms, since it coats roads and power lines in a solid glaze.

🧊 Record Stone: The largest hailstone ever officially measured fell near Vivian, South Dakota in 2010 — 8 inches across and nearly 2 pounds, requiring an updraft over 175 km/h to stay aloft.
