Light diffraction, rainbows, halos, and sun dogs explained.

When sunlight enters a spherical raindrop, it refracts, internally reflects, and refracts again — dispersing into its spectral components. A primary rainbow appears at 42° from the anti-solar point; a secondary at 51° with reversed colors and Alexander's dark band between.

Ice halos are produced by hexagonal ice crystals in cirrus clouds. The 22° halo is most common. Sun dogs (parhelia) appear as bright spots 22° to either side of the sun when flat plate crystals are horizontally oriented.

🌈 Double Rainbow: In the secondary bow, light undergoes two internal reflections, reversing the color order.
