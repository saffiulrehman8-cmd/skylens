Fog &
Visibility

Radiation, advection, and sea fog — when clouds touch the ground.

Related Topics

Atmospheric Optics
Monsoon Systems

Fog is a cloud at ground level — a suspension of tiny water droplets reducing horizontal visibility below 1,000m. Radiation fog forms on calm, clear nights when the ground cools to the dew point. Advection fog forms when warm, moist air flows over a cooler surface — classically seen along coastlines like San Francisco.

Freezing fog deposits rime ice when droplets are supercooled below 0°C. The Great Smog of London (December 1952) caused an estimated 4,000–12,000 excess deaths.

💧 Fog Collection: Specialized mesh collectors in coastal deserts can harvest hundreds of liters per day from passing fog banks.
