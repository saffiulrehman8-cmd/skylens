Tornadoes

Vortex mechanics, Fujita scale, and tornado alley explained.

A tornado is a violently rotating column of air in contact with both the Earth's surface and a cumulonimbus cloud. Most significant tornadoes spawn from supercell thunderstorms via interaction of environmental wind shear with strong convective updrafts, tilting horizontal vorticity into the vertical.

The Enhanced Fujita (EF) scale rates tornado intensity from EF0 (~105 km/h) to EF5 (>322 km/h). The US experiences ~1,200 tornadoes annually.

🌪 El Reno (2013): The widest tornado ever recorded at 4.2km wide — an EF5 with winds estimated at 295 km/h.
