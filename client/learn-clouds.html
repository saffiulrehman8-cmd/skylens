Cloud
Taxonomy

From high-altitude Cirrus to the storm-bearing Cumulonimbus.

Related Topics

Atmospheric Optics
Fog & Visibility

Clouds are classified into ten main genera by altitude and form. High-level clouds above 6,000m — Cirrus, Cirrostratus, Cirrocumulus — consist almost entirely of ice crystals. Delicate Cirrus filaments often signal approaching frontal systems 24–48 hours in advance.

Mid-level clouds (2,000–6,000m) include the patchy Altocumulus and the layered Altostratus. Low Stratus and Stratocumulus sit below 2,000m. The towering Cumulonimbus spans all altitude layers — responsible for thunderstorms, hail, and extreme precipitation.

🌩 Cumulonimbus: A single Cb cell can release over 500,000 tons of water and generate updrafts exceeding 160 km/h — spanning from surface to the stratosphere.
